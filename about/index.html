About

Prioritizing Boundaries: a boundary is not a cage. It is a line you can see, and a door that opens when it should.

What we do

One method for every system, whatever it is made of.

  • Find the boundary. Every boundary carries friction. Positive friction releases; negative friction constrains. Where the system says yes and no to a person. What it may do to you, and what you may do back.
  • Make it visible. Put the line where anyone can see it, in the system's own words, not ours.
  • Never let the system grade itself. A law does not say whether it is fair. An AI does not write its own report card. A study does not confirm its own claim.
  • Leave the evidence where anyone can reach it. The filing, the text, the paper, the log. Open the same thing we opened and see the same thing we saw.

Three questions, asked of every system.

1

Does it protect or exploit identity?

A thing is what its own record says it is, not what it looks like or calls itself. No record, no entry.

2

Does it provide transparency?

What was done can be seen afterwards without trusting anyone's summary. The method is public and the code is open.

3

Does it hold integrity to the whole?

Nothing changes quietly. Corrections are dated and the old version stays.

How

Prioritizing Boundaries: a boundary is not a cage. It is a line you can see, and a door that opens when it should.

  • Everyone is read alike. A church, a company, an agency, a law, an AI and a paper get the same questions and the same standard.
  • The questions are plain. Can you say no? Can you be heard? Can you appeal? Who wrote it? Can an outsider check it? Who answers when it fails?
  • Friction is the measure. Pressure with a way out is healthy. A door that should open and does not is the finding.
  • A missing answer is an answer. It is said plainly, never filled in with something close.
  • Written as it happens. Every change is logged, so rewriting history shows.
  • For people, not against anyone. The evidence speaks. The page never accuses. Anyone named may respond, and the response is posted in full.

How we publish

Signals for review, not verdicts.

  1. Attributed to the evidence. Every finding names the source it rests on, with a link.
  2. Checked twice. Work is drafted, then independently re-checked: sources re-opened, wording screened, limits stated.
  3. Made with AI assistance, and why. Priority Nexus is run by one person, its founder, who is autistic and has ADHD (AuDHD) and is a woman in a man's world. The programs and the AI do the reading, the counting and the drafting at a scale one person could not reach alone. The rules they follow, the records they may use and the decision to publish are hers. It is said here so readers can weigh it, the same way every other interest is said.
  4. Right to respond. No one is asked to comment before a report goes out. Anyone named may respond afterwards, and the response is posted in full, under their name, the day it arrives.
  5. Open corrections. Found an error? Post it here or write to us. Every e-mail is read by a program the moment it arrives and the check begins; the message is posted at once, in full, under the person or company it says it is from, and stays that way unless the check shows otherwise. Contact details are never posted. Corrections are dated, and earlier versions stay available.
