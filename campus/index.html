A community within a community

This is a concept, not a place that exists. A campus or a hotel is turned into a community that meets the needs of everyone living there, trades work and services with local businesses, and opens a market to the public. Every rule in it has to pass the same nine questions we ask of a law, with the resident as the person being asked.

The principle. It is on a volunteer basis. No one has to live there, work or volunteer. Many people will need time to heal, in a setting with as little outside pressure as possible. When they arrive, the goal is that they receive abundance. If they hurt or harm, the answer is never to take, but to give, to provide.

Each rule below is followed by what published research says about it, for and against. Open any line to read it.

What it looks like

Day work and temp work are there to sign up for. Local businesses that are short on money get the help they need, and pay with what they can exchange: goods, services, a share of what they make. That fills the kitchen and the rooms. Residents sell their own goods in the market, where the public can see what the community has to offer.

The nine rules

One night in the kitchen

A man comes in off the street. That night he takes food from the kitchen.

Nobody takes his room. Nobody takes anything.

A message goes to the rooms nearest his:

Room 14 is in need. Please bring abundance.

It names the room, not the man, and it does not say what he did. The same message goes out when someone is sick, or has just arrived with nothing.

His neighbors fill his room with food and warmth.
